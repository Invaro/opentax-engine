# Corpus v0.43.0

## What's new

- QBI per business, with aggregation. The § 199A deduction now follows Form 8995-A with one column per business. Above the threshold, each business has its own W-2 wage and UBIA limit and its own SSTB status, instead of one limit for the whole return. List up to five businesses separately with `qbiAdditionalBusinesses` and `qbiBusiness2Income` through `qbiBusiness6Income`, each with its own `W2Wages`, `UBIA`, `IsSSTB` and `AggregationGroup`. What isn't listed stays in the main business, which keeps `qbiW2Wages`, `qbiUBIA` and `businessIsSSTB`.
- Two self-employed spouses. On a joint return, the second spouse's Schedule C is its own business, with `spouseQbiW2Wages`, `spouseQbiUBIA` and `spouseBusinessIsSSTB`. Above the threshold this used to refuse; it now computes.
- Aggregation (Schedule B). Businesses with the same group number add their QBI, W-2 wages and UBIA and are limited as one. Aggregating an SSTB is refused (Reg. § 1.199A-4(b)(1)(iii)).
- Loss netting (Schedule C). A business's QBI loss, plus last year's carryforward (`qbiLossCarryforward`), is shared among the profitable businesses in proportion to their QBI. Next year's carryforward is `us.federal.qbi.loss_carryforward`.
- The 2026 $400 minimum now needs $1,000 of QBI from qualified businesses. An SSTB past the phase-in range isn't one, so it now gets $0 instead of $400.

Apart from that minimum, every existing amount is the same. Returns with business income list two more assumptions, `qbiAdditionalBusinesses` and `qbiLossCarryforward`, so a caller that refuses defaults is asked for both. core and compose are unchanged from v0.42.0. solve is 0.1.1 with no code change: its tarball no longer lists the corpus, which it only uses in tests.
